# English editorial guide

## Voice and information order

Write as a construction contractor describing recorded project work: factual,
restrained and specific. Start with what the project is and where it is. Then
explain the documented scope, sequence or distribution. Keep interpretation
limited to what `facts.json`, the approved page source or an explicitly visible
image feature supports.

Use active verbs for recorded work (`delivered`, `executed`, `completed`,
`installed`, `coordinated`) and neutral linking verbs for project description
(`includes`, `contains`, `is located in`). Do not use a luxury-property voice,
architecture-critic language or generic AI corporate claims.

Write for a construction contractor, not an architecture magazine, urban-design
journal or property developer. Prefer location, project type, verified
quantities, floors, documented scope, construction status and contractor role.
Avoid active frontage, urban integration, design intention, vertical composition,
facade composition, master plan, architectural language, spatial narrative,
landmark, iconic and contemporary lifestyle language.

## Field rules

- `content.summary` is one short overview. Include location and project type;
  keep detailed parking breakdowns and typical-floor counts in structured detail.
- `content.description` explains scope or spatial distribution in up to two
  paragraphs. Summary, description and FAQ must not repeat one another.
- `content.authority` states delivery status or documented contractor scope only.
  Omit it when the facts do not establish contractor responsibility.
- `content.details`, `facilityGroups` and `nearby` are factual structures. Keep
  labels, IDs, paths, acronyms and numbers stable.
- FAQ questions must be useful and answers must add information. Never paste a
  hero summary as an FAQ answer.
- Alt text names the visible subject; captions add short project context. Do
  not describe hidden systems or infer completion from a photograph.

## Banned or unsupported language

Do not use `architectural excellence`, `landmark development`, `optimises urban
density`, `creates an active frontage`, `resolves site constraints`, `complex
vertical coordination`, `pushes the portfolio upward`, `premium`, `high-density`,
`free promotional housing` as a promotional claim, or `parking entre-sols`
unless the exact wording is supported and the field contract permits it. Never
turn `VRD`, `TCE`, LPA/LPL/LPP or a French source fragment into a marketing
synonym.

## SEO

Use one project name, location and natural project type. Do not repeat a
keyword list, add unsupported superlatives or duplicate the summary verbatim.
Keep title and description within their field contracts.

## Literal-translation failures to avoid

Avoid French syntax such as `parking entre-sols`, `corps d'etat` or
`promotionnel` in English. Do not preserve sentence order when it creates
unnatural coordination. Prefer: “The scope covered the recorded secondary
works for two commercial centres in Douira.”
